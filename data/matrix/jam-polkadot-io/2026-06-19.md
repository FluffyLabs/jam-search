Listening to the fireside chat for JAM, with the chat focused on the economics of the JAM token. I had a question w.r.t. the need for a new JAM token rather than mapping the dot token via some dynamic pricing model.

I always thought this “resource allotment” economic problem is solved nicely with the model Crust Network presents in its economic white paper, and I would love to hear critique of why this model isn’t viable?

The tl:dr is that
- you target x% usage (for example x=50% for Crust)
- when the overall usage is *> x%*, the price per unit multiplies by some alpha every time a unit of usage is requested
- when the overall usage is *< x%*, the price per unit multiplies by some 1/alpha every time a unit of usage is requested

The potential issues I could think of are
- it is a bit of a fuzzy economic guarantee that usage doesn’t exceed available resources
- it relies on there being an amount of unused resources which isn’t perfectly efficient
- there are some technical/implementation complexities which I am unaware of?

If by dynamic pricing you mean that some amount of DOT would buy you a different amount of storage at different points in time, this is not really compatible with or at least would significantly complicate the storage deposit mechanism in JAM. At the moment there is a fixed function between the balance of a service and how much data it can store. If this function is time-dependent, what happens when the price goes up and a service no longer has a large enough deposit to cover its storage costs?
