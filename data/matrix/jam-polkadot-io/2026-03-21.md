**finsig** (2026-03-21T18:30:00.000Z) [msgid:$VVDYjMHz7JYlUtU0KS8srJASc_kzj-7HpiaP76C3_Uw]:
Social proof makes sense but I respectfully disagree with it trumping every code review. I opted to figure things out on my own rather than ask questions. I knew that other teams already passed vectors so whatever I was stuck on was something specific to my interpretation of the GP. My questions would have been more along the lines of “how is this supposed to work” or “how is this typically implemented” rather than “I think it should work this way, what I am missing”. 

And also, from the outset, in 2024 at least, I recall the contest language saying that publicly stating intent to participate etc was optional and not required. Also care was taken (public archive etc) to ensure that private channels weren’t created that disadvantage others. Attendance of the in-person Thailand and Portugal events would have been based on timing and personal circumstances. 

A more egalitarian alternative might be to have teams submit some things they got stuck on and how they worked through them. Implementers who have passed M1 should easily be able to recall running across the exact same obscure things.

IMO significant weight should be given to the reasoning expressed through code over the timeframe of development. For example, it should be clear if one sincerely reasoned forward from the GP vs taking a TDD approach backward from the test vectors.
