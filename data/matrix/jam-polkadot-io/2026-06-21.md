**kianenigma** (2026-06-21T12:41:00.000Z) [msgid:$gkQNh_F1uptU_MN7lvFyESUvgE-QYjo5ld2aevWPq_k]:
I think it is best to see the full talk as it requires some context, but what I will rephrase what I learnt as best as I can: 

1. Imagine Polkadot's relay chain and parachain's relationship. There is no concern related to a parachain causing overhead on the state of the relay chain -- the protocol simply doesn't allow this. Each parachain's overhead on the relay chain state is fixed (some hashes + wasm code), and accounted for by a deposit
2. In JAM, this is fundamentally different. A JAM service can write an arbitrary amount to the JAM chain's state in its accumulate, and therefore a new area of accounting is needed

In the talk a few proposals were mentioned around this, all revolving around the idea of using tokens (not necessarily a "crypto" token but think more broadly as a "access token" used in CS before any blockchain) to represent them: CORE for any core of JAM and JAMKB for any KB of the JAM's state, and they would both be strict utility tokens with supplies that exactly represent the underlying utility (a core or a KB of state). So if 21 GiB is the presumed max capacity of the JAM's state, there would be ~21M JAMKB tokens ever created.  

You could also continue to use DOT for them, but the fact that DOT has a different supply + other utilities + its demand depends on many other things than e.g. wishing to use a JAM core, as I understood, means it will inevitably be a suboptimal design.

Both JAM and CORE if created will be minted and controlled by the DOTDAO (Polkadot Gov) and then handed out as the DAO decides. 

I personally have some questions about whether this should have a "use it or lose it" mechanism to prevent hoarding.
