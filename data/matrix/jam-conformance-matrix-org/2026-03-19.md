**tomusdrw** (2026-03-19T09:13:00.000Z) [msgid:$UIbdkHzwJjYGPfgOM_JNgEMTcDF3gsFzIfOzDdgZy0E]:
To make it more heated 😈: Can we just drop the natural number encoding everywhere and define fixed lengths? While it makes sense in PVM to have small, canonical representation of the program blob. I think everywhere else it's net negative. Fixed-width encoding allows pre-computing sizes of all structures (obviously without variable length elements), allows preallocation, offset-lookups, etc. Fixed-width would also allow much better (human readable) error messages for decoding errors. Each implementation needs to inflate the "canonical encoding" to their own pointer-aligned layout anyway because of this, while fixed-width would allow no-copy reads much easier I feel.
If the goal of natural numbers encoding is:
1. to shave off few bytes in networking? - then maybe it's better to just introduce fast (streaming) compression instead?
2. to shave off few bytes in database? - then there are much bigger gains here and it falls outside of the consensus
3. to simplify the gray paper? - that's arguable as well - as we see it already created at least 2 rounds of misunderstandings afair (ticket attempt, service ids) and it's just a matter of explicitly stating ranges for some numbers
