## Comment by @alxmirap

> Regarding seed based shuffle generation, $\mathcal{E}^{-1}_4(\mathcal{H}(h \frown \mathcal{E}_4(\lfloor \frac{i}{8}\rfloor))_{4i \bmod 32 \dots+4})$ => if $4i \bmod 32 \geq 29 $ then we have $|(\mathcal{H}(h \frown \mathcal{E}_4(\lfloor \frac{i}{8}\rfloor))_{4i \bmod 32 \dots+4})| &lt; 4$. How do we "complete it" to make an array of 4 bytes ? Do we just put zeros ?

Hello @celadari. To address just this point, although $i$ can range over the naturals, notice that it is multiplied by 4 before taking the modulo. So, the case you mention will never happen. To see this:

any value of $i$ can be put in one of 8 residue classes, according to $i \bmod 8$, so $i$ can be:

* $8k$
* $8k + 1$
* $\ldots$
* $8k + 7$

And consequently, $4i$ can be:

* $32k$
* $32k + 4$
* $\ldots$
* $32k + 28$

Since the $32k$ term disappears in the modulo operation, we have that the result is a multiple of 4 between $0$ and $28$ inclusive and so you can always take 4 consecutive bytes from the hash expression.

I hope that helps.
